1 The Big Picture: What SAP SD Does

Understanding the end-to-end Order-to-Cash process flow.

The Core Paradigm

SAP SD is not a random collection of disconnected screens. Saving a single transaction automatically updates downstream logistics, updates inventory levels in real-time, generates financial postings, and drives availability requirements.

Standard SD Document Flow

Business Step SAP Document / Object Standard Code (ECC) Main Business Question Answered
1. Customer Offer Request Inquiry / Quotation IN / QT What can we offer, and under what terms?
2. Order Placement Sales Order TA / OR What exact products/services must we supply?
3. Shipping Prep Outbound Delivery LF What items will be picked, packed, and staged?
4. Goods Movement Goods Issue (PGI) mvt 601 Has inventory physically left our ownership/control?
5. Invoicing Billing Document F2 What final monetary amount should be charged?
6. Accounting Integration FI Accounting Document RV Posting What accounts receivable & revenue postings occur?

2 Chapter 1: Enterprise Structure & Organization

The foundational backbone governing commercial and logistical rules.

Golden Rule of SD
Sales Area = Sales Org + Dist. Channel + Division

Every sales transaction must belong to a valid Sales Area. A customer master can maintain separate payment terms, prices, and shipping rules for different Sales Areas.

SO

Sales Organization

Highest commercial selling unit. Responsible for product liability, negotiating terms, and commercial terms. Assigned to one Company Code.

DC

Distribution Channel

The route to market (e.g., Wholesale 10, Retail 20, Internet Sales). Allows different pricing and minimum order policies for identical materials.

DV

Division

Product line or service group (e.g., Spare Parts 01, Machinery 02). Used to perform product-line analysis and assign partial delivery agreements.

PL

Plant & Shipping Point

Plant: Physical location for inventory storage, ATP stock checks, and procurement.
Shipping Point: Location that handles picking, packing, and dispatch.

3 Chapter 2: Master Data & Configuration Controls

Controlling customer behavior, material rules, and default priorities.

Account Group Controls & Units of Measure

The Account Group controls how customer master screens behave. Fields can be set to: Suppress, Display, Optional, or Required. This guarantees data quality across various partner types.

Units of Measure Hierarchy Example
Base Unit
1 Piece

Fundamental unit for stock valuation & inventory balance.

Sales Unit
1 Box (= 5 Pieces)

Proposed default unit during order entry.

Delivery Unit
Multiples of 30 Pcs

Restricts delivery creation to exact pallet/crate multiples.

Customer-Material Information Record & Master Data Priority

When SAP looks for delivering plants, delivery priorities, or customer-specific material identifiers, it follows a strict 3-tier priority sequence:

1. Cust-Mat Info Record (VD51)
2. Customer Master (VD01/XD01)
3. Material Master (MM01)

* Example: Customer-specific plant defaults in VD51 will always override general plant defaults in the Material Master!

4 Chapter 3: Sales Document Mechanics

Understanding the 3-level sales document architecture & determination chains.

The 3 Levels of a Sales Document

1. Header Level VBAK

Valid for the whole document.

Control: Sales Doc Type (e.g. TA)
Data: Sold-to, Terms, Sales Area, Currency, Header Blocks.
2. Item Level VBAP

Valid for individual line items.

Control: Item Category (e.g. TAN)
Data: Material, Line Qty, Pricing, Plant, Costing, Delivery Relevance.
3. Schedule Line VBEP

Valid for delivery dates & splits.

Control: Schedule Line Category (CP)
Data: Confirmed Qty, Goods Issue Dates, Movement Types (601), MRP requirements.

The Master Determination Chain

How SAP automatically figures out what each order line item does and how logistics will handle fulfillment:

1. Inputs:
Doc Type (OR) + Item Cat Group (NORM) + Item Usage
2. Determines Item Category: TAN (Standard Item)
3. Item Category (TAN) + MRP Type (PD): CP (MRP Schedule Line)

5 Available-to-Promise (ATP) Mechanics

Understanding fulfillment feasibility, scheduling logic, and cumulative availability checks.

Core SD Fulfillment Question

"Can we fulfill this order? If yes, how much can we deliver and when?"

When a sales order is entered, SAP instantly executes an ATP check against available stock, incoming purchase orders, production receipts, and existing customer commitments to calculate accurate delivery promises.

The 4-Step ATP Order Flow

1
Requested Date & Qty: Customer specifies desired delivery date and quantity.
2
Backward Scheduling: System computes required Material Availability Date.
3
Stock & Receipts Check: Evaluates stock balances and scheduled stock receipts.
4
System Confirmation: Proposes Full Confirmation, Partial Split, or Proposed Future Date.

Levels of ATP Check

ATP checks can be executed at the Plant Level (combining all storage locations) or restricted to a specific Storage Location Level based on customizing.

Plant 1010 Total Stock 150 Pcs
SLoc A
50 Pcs
SLoc B
100 Pcs

* If check is set to SLoc A, an order for 70 Pcs will fail even though Plant 1010 has 150 Pcs!

Material Availability Date & Delivery Scheduling

SAP checks ATP against the calculated Material Availability Date rather than the requested delivery date.

Backward Scheduling Sequence
Requested Delivery Date
e.g. July 20
Transportation Lead Time
Transit Duration
Picking & Packing
Warehouse Handling
Material Availability Date
Check Date (July 17)
Forward Scheduling Trigger

If stock is unavailable on the calculated Material Availability Date (e.g. July 17), SAP switches to Forward Scheduling. It finds the next earliest date stock will be available (or uses Replenishment Lead Time) and calculates a new proposed delivery date for the customer.

Cumulative ATP Quantity Formula

ATP does not merely check current warehouse stock. It computes future availability dynamically:

Current Physical Stock + 80
Incoming Purchase Orders / Planned Receipts + 60
Existing Confirmed Sales Orders - 0
Cumulative ATP Quantity = 140 Units

When a new order for 70 units is placed, it is confirmed, and the remaining Cumulative ATP quantity reduces to 70 units for future checks.

First-Come First-Served & Delivery Agreements

First-Come, First-Served Reservation

Order A reserves 70 units. Subsequent Order B (requesting 100 units) will only find 70 units available, forcing partial confirmation or forward delivery proposal.

Complete Delivery

All line items must be confirmed together. If Item B is delayed, Item A is held until both are ready.

Partial Delivery

Partial quantities are shipped as soon as available, with remaining split into backorders.

S/4HANA

aATP & Alternative-Based Confirmation (ABC)

In SAP S/4HANA, Advanced ATP (aATP) introduces intelligent automated substitution logic:

ABC Automatic Substitution Hierarchy:
Requested: Plant 1010 Out of Stock
Auto-Check Alternative
Alternate: Plant 1020 Confirmed Stock ✅

aATP automatically checks alternative plants, storage locations, or substitute materials before rejecting an order date.

ATP vs. MRP: Classic Interview Question

ATP (Available-to-Promise)
Answers: "Can I promise this order to the customer right now?"

Validates immediate sales feasibility without triggering supply creation.

MRP (Material Requirements Planning)
Answers: "How do I procure or produce the missing stock?"

When ATP identifies a shortage, MRP generates Purchase Requisitions or Planned/Production Orders to replenish stock.

Top 5 Certification & Project Takeaways for ATP

Must Know
Material Availability Date: ATP checks availability against the calculated material availability date, NOT the requested delivery date directly.
Forward Scheduling: If stock is unavailable during backward scheduling, SAP triggers forward scheduling to propose realistic dates.
Cumulative ATP Quantity: Considers physical stock + planned receipts - commitments to determine promise quantity.
Replenishment Lead Time (RLT): If stock is absent, SAP can confirm stock at the end of the material's procurement lead time.

8 Practice Test & Flashcard Checkpoint

Verify your mastery of key concepts with instant answer reveals.

Q1. Why is a Sales Area a combination rather than a single unit?

Why combine Sales Org, Distribution Channel, and Division?

Answer: It allows a customer to have different prices, payment terms, shipping conditions, and sales offices depending on the route to market and product line.
Q2. Base vs. Sales vs. Delivery Units

Stocked in pcs, sold in boxes of 5, delivered in multiples of 10. Identify each.

Answer: Base Unit = Piece; Sales Unit = Box (1 box = 5 pcs); Delivery Unit = 10 pcs (or 2 boxes).
Q3. Which object controls line pricing & delivery relevance?

Does the Header Doc Type or Item Category control line behavior?

Answer: The Item Category (e.g., TAN) controls pricing and delivery relevance for line items.
Q4. Plant vs. Shipping Point

Why are they distinct organizational entities?

Answer: A plant is a location for stock valuation and MRP checks. A shipping point is the physical loading spot for deliveries; one plant can have multiple shipping points (e.g. Express vs Standard).
